# Character generator

`qtmesh character` / MCP `generate_character` / Inspector "Character Generator" build a rigged human from two open data sets:

* **MHR** (Momentum Human Rig, Apache-2.0): 18,439-vertex body, 45 identity shape modes, 127-joint skeleton + skin weights.
* **ICT-FaceKit** (MIT): head with 100 identity modes and 51 ARKit expression shapes; teeth, tongue, eyes and lashes are separate parts.

## Head
By default the character keeps the MHR body's **own head** (no seam, no cut). The 51 ARKit expression shapes are built offline
(`scripts/build-character-assets.py`) by transferring the ICT-FaceKit shapes onto the MHR head and expressing them with MHR's own
72 face-expression vectors (bounded ridge least squares); the jaw shapes are the real skinned motion of the `c_jaw` joint, the
eyelid shapes are transferred directly in a mask around each eye. The mouth interior (gums, tongue, teeth) is ICT's; with the native head the eyeballs are generated procedurally (see **Eyes** below), and only the ICT head (`--ict-head`) keeps ICT's own eyeballs. MHR's eyes are sculpted shut, so
with **Open eyes** on (the default; `--closed-eyes` / MCP `open_eyes:false` / the panel checkbox turn it off) the thin layer between the lids is removed
(the triangle list was picked by hand, `scripts/mhr_eye_layer_faces.json`) and the eyeballs show; eye colour only has an effect then.
The eyeballs and the mouth interior are seated relative to landmarks on the face (the eyelid layer and the lip line), so they follow every body/head shape.
**Hair** (optional bundle `hair_character.qmcb`, built by `scripts/build-hair-assets.py` from MakeHuman's CC0 system assets; `--hair STYLE` / `--list-hair`, MCP `hair`, the panel's Hair picker; native head only). MakeHuman fits hair to its own base mesh, so each style is converted ONCE, offline: its vertices are evaluated on the raw MakeHuman base from the `.mhclo` binding, taken through a warp of MakeHuman's head onto the MHR head (similarity ICP + RBF rounds, ~2 mm mean on the cranium) and BOUND to the MHR neutral surface (triangle + barycentric weights + the offset in the triangle's frame, kept a little above the scalp). At runtime the binding is evaluated on the shaped body, so the style follows every head shape, and the skin weights are blended from the bound vertices. The material is alpha-tested (cards, both sides). The bundle is read from the character assets folder when present (ten styles, ~11 MB, 1024² textures); it is hosted next to the body/head bundles (`character/hair_character.qmcb` + `LICENSE-MakeHuman-hair.txt`, pinned digests; an OPTIONAL download: if it fails the hair picker is simply absent). **Hair colour**: the bundle stores each texture as a neutral greyscale SHADING map (`scripts/hair-shading-maps.py` turns MakeHuman's brown textures into maps normalised on their median luminance and clears the `www.makehuman.org` logo burned into some of them), and `HairTint.{h,cpp}` tints it at build time with a three-stop gradient (shadow = colour x 0.55, mid = the colour, highlight = colour x 1.45 + 0.06), so one texture serves every colour. Nine presets sampled from the reference hair-colour chart (black, dark_brown, brown, light_brown, blonde, platinum_blonde, red, gray, white) or any `#rrggbb`: `--hair-colour NAME|#rrggbb` / `--list-hair-colours`, MCP `hair_colour`, the panel's swatch row + hex field.

**Skin texture.** The body gets a procedural albedo (on by default; `--no-skin-texture` / MCP `skin_texture:false` / the panel checkbox turn it off): tone patches, blush, freckles (more on fair skin) and pores around the skin colour, baked in the body's own UV layout (`SkinTexture.{h,cpp}`, 1024², PNG in `<AppData>/generated_textures/`, exported with the glTF). The noise is sampled at each texel's 3D surface position, so it is continuous across UV seams and has the same grain everywhere. Only for the native head (the ICT head has another UV layout). The **lips** are painted around the lip-seam landmarks (a warm colour derived from the skin tone, fine vertical creases, a darker seam line) and the **eyebrows** above each eye's lid-layer landmark (an arched band of hair strokes, thick at the inner end and tapering; colour from the skin tone; only with open eyes). Both are sized from the landmark vertices, so they follow the head shape. **Body hair** (`--body-hair 0..1`, MCP `body_hair`, the panel slider; default 0.5, 0 = none): a faint hair-coloured shade plus a stubble of darker texel-sized dots, densest on shins and forearms, moderate on chest and abdomen, sparse on hands and feet, none on the head. The density per body vertex is the skin-weighted density of the joints that drive it (`bodyHairDensityOfJoint`), interpolated per texel. The **areolae and nipples** are two soft discs (areola 1.6 cm, nipple 0.5 cm, scaled by the chest width) with a fine bumpy grain; their centres are the few mean-body vertices nearest to an anatomical point (11.6 cm off the midline, 74 % of the body height, front of the chest), read back on the shaped body, so no extra bundle data is needed. Colour comes from the skin tone.

**Lids.** The eyes rest slightly lower than the sculpted open shape (it looked too wide), `eyeBlinkLeft/Right` now close the eye completely (the upper lid skin turns about the eyeball, so it never sinks into it) and `eyeWide*` still reaches the old fully open look. The lashes turn with the lid.
**Eyelashes.** A curved alpha-card strip along each lid margin (upper and lower, `--no-eyelashes` / MCP `eyelashes:false` / the panel checkbox turn them off; native head with open eyes). The strips are rooted on the lid-margin vertices of the opening, so they follow the blink, squint and wide-eye shapes automatically; the strand texture is painted in code in the `lash` colour.
**Eyes (procedural).** With the native head and open eyes the eyeballs are generated in code (`EyeTexture.{h,cpp}`): a unit sphere with a polar UV map and a 1024² texture painted from the eye colour — sclera with faint veins, a fibrous iris with a pale collarette ring, crypts and a dark limbal ring, and a pupil. Any eye colour works (not a fixed set), there is no asset to license, and it ships with the editor. The eyeballs carry the eight `eyeLook{In,Out,Up,Down}{Left,Right}` ARKit shapes as morph targets (each eyeball turns about its centre, about 30°), so the iris follows face capture / lipsync clips that drive them. Two extra channels, `pupilDilate` and `pupilConstrict`, move the vertex rings around the pupil (the painted pupil and the iris texture follow the geometry, so the pupil grows / shrinks like a real one). The ICT head (`--ict-head`) keeps ICT's own eyeballs. A textured eye of your own is opt-in: set `QTMESH_CHARACTER_EYE_OBJ` to an eyeball OBJ (a UV sphere whose iris pole is on -Y; it is rotated to face forward and scaled to the seated eyeball) and `QTMESH_CHARACTER_EYE_TEX` (or `QTMESH_CHARACTER_EYE_DIR`) to a directory holding `iris_blue|brown|grey|green|hazel|dark.png` (`scripts/build-eye-iris-textures.py`); nothing is picked up implicitly, and such assets are never hosted or bundled (check the licence of the one you use: marketplace licences usually forbid redistributing the files).
`--ict-head` / MCP `head: "ict"` selects the older ICT head stitched on at the neck (native ARKit shapes, eyes, visible seam).

## Output
Body and head are separate submeshes (`body`, `head_skin`, `head_mouth`, `head_teeth`, `head_sclera`, `head_iris`, `head_lashes`) so
materials and hair attach per part. The skeleton is the standard 52-joint Mixamo-style set (names from `SamBody::mhrAlias`), so
text-to-motion, retargeting and mocap work unchanged. Every part carries all 51 ARKit morph targets (zero-delta dummies where a part
has no deformation) so glTF export keeps the target names.

## Pipeline notes
* The head is scaled/anchored to the body by chin + head-top; a triangle strip stitches the neck (the body loses its head-dominated and neck-column vertices above the cut).
* The sclera front cap is removed so the iris colour shows.
* Undriven MHR twist/helper joints fold into the nearest kept ancestor; bones are world-aligned (identity orientations) — without that, limbs collapse under animation.
* Assets: `QTMESH_CHARACTER_DIR` or `<AppData>/ai_models/character` (`mhr_character.qmcb`, `ict_character.qmcb`, licence texts), downloaded on first use with pinned SHA-256s. Build them with `scripts/build-character-assets.py`; host with `scripts/upload-character-assets.sh`.
